Student Housing in Turin: A Complete Guide

ISOT Editorial Team • 2025-03-05 • ISOT Turin Guide

How to Find Student Housing in Turin: A Complete Guide

Turin, a vibrant student city, is home to thousands of international students studying at institutions like Politecnico di Torino and Università degli Studi di Torino. Finding the right housing is crucial for a comfortable and productive academic life. This guide will walk you through the best options, costs, and essential tips for securing student accommodation in Turin.

1. Types of Student Accommodation

a) University Dormitories

Both Politecnico di Torino and Università di Torino offer student dormitories through organizations like EDISU Piemonte. These accommodations are affordable and often include utilities, Wi-Fi, and communal areas.

  • Pros: Affordable, social environment, close to university campuses.

  • Cons: Limited availability, application process required.

  • How to apply: Check the EDISU website (www.edisu.piemonte.it) and apply early.

b) Private Student Residences

If you prefer a more modern and comfortable setting, private student residences are a great option. Some popular ones include:

  • CX Turin (CampusX)

  • Aparto Student Turin

  • Camplus These residences offer amenities like gyms, study rooms, and social events but come at a higher price.

  • Pros: Fully furnished, modern, student-friendly services.

  • Cons: More expensive than university dorms.

c) Shared Apartments (“Appartamenti in condivisione”)

Many students choose to rent a private apartment and share it with roommates. Websites and Facebook groups help find available rooms.

  • Pros: More independence, flexible options.

  • Cons: Utility costs may not be included, longer searching process.

  • Best Websites:

    • Idealista (www.idealista.it)

    • Bakeca (www.bakeca.it)

    • Uniaffitti (www.uniaffitti.it)

    • Facebook Groups (e.g., “Affitti studenti Torino”)

d) Homestays (Living with an Italian Family)

For those who want to improve their Italian and experience local culture, a homestay with an Italian family can be an option.

  • Pros: Cultural immersion, meals often included.

  • Cons: Less privacy, house rules may apply.

  • Where to find: Websites like Homestay.com and local Facebook groups.

2. Average Rent Prices in Turin

Prices vary depending on location and accommodation type. Here’s a general overview:

  • University Dorms: €250–€400/month

  • Shared Apartments: €300–€500/month per room

  • Private Studios: €500–€800/month

  • Private Student Residences: €500–€900/month

Additional costs such as utilities (€50–€100/month) and internet (€20–€30/month) should be considered.

3. Best Neighborhoods for Students

Choosing the right area is essential for a good living experience. Here are some of the best student-friendly neighborhoods:

  • San Salvario – Lively area with bars and restaurants, close to the city center.

  • Vanchiglia – Artistic and youthful, near Università di Torino.

  • San Donato – Affordable and well-connected to public transport.

  • Cenisia – Near Politecnico di Torino, popular with students.

  • Lingotto – Cheaper rents, good metro access.

4. How to Secure an Apartment in Turin

Step 1: Start Early

Finding housing in Turin can be competitive, especially before the academic year begins. Start searching at least 2–3 months before arrival.

Step 2: Use Reliable Platforms

Avoid scams by using trusted websites like Idealista, Bakeca, and EDISU. Always visit the apartment in person or request a video tour before making any payment.

Step 3: Prepare Necessary Documents

Landlords may require the following:

  • Passport or residence permit

  • Proof of university enrollment

  • Guarantor details (if applicable)

  • One or two months’ rent as a deposit

Step 4: Sign a Contract

Ensure you get a written contract (“contratto di locazione”) that specifies:

  • Rent amount and included expenses

  • Deposit terms

  • Duration of the contract

  • House rules (if any)

5. Tips to Avoid Scams

  • Never send money upfront without verifying the property and landlord.

  • Check for official registration (ask for a “codice fiscale” and contract).

  • Avoid unrealistic offers (too cheap compared to average market prices).

  • Use official rental agencies for added security.

6. Conclusion

Finding student housing in Turin requires planning and research, but with the right approach, you can secure a comfortable and affordable place. Whether you choose university dorms, shared apartments, or private residences, be sure to start early, use trusted resources, and always verify before committing. Good luck with your search, and enjoy your student life in Turin!
